# RuneScape, Jagex and the global gaming industry — research conversation record

**As of:** 1 November 2026 (Australia/Sydney)  
**Scope:** Research-only reference recording the key subjects covered in the 1 November 2026 conversation: executive governance, Jagex ownership, private equity, Treasure Hunter, RuneScape's IP value, franchise ownership, corporate scale, acquisitions, and critic scores.  
**Status:** Source-backed synthesis with clearly marked estimates and unresolved claims. **Not** financial advice, a formal business valuation, exhaustive ownership register, or live player-count study.  
**JoshRS boundary:** This is context about Jagex and gaming-industry IP. It does **not** authorise the reuse of Jagex trademarks, game assets, code, audio or characters in JoshRS; it makes no changes to the active revision-377 game.

## Executive summary

- Jagex is the RuneScape developer and franchise steward. **Jon Bellamy** became its CEO in March 2025; **Marc Allera** is the chair of its board; investment funds managed/advised by **CVC** and **Haveli Investments** agreed to buy Jagex from Carlyle in February 2024. The published announcement did **not** disclose transaction terms.
- **Brian Sheth** founded Haveli and is its chief investment officer. He discussed investing alongside CVC in Jagex at the acquisition announcement. That is evidence of strategic involvement **not** proof of the frequency or participants of private meetings.
- RuneScape 3's paid random-reward **Treasure Hunter** system ended on 19 January 2026 following a vote with **124,985 votes**. Key purchases ended on 12 January and 225 direct-XP/skilling items were withdrawn.
- Public comparisons often confuse **franchise IP**, **entire-company enterprise value**, **acquisition consideration**, **annual revenue**, **assets under management**, and **market capitalisation**. These are different measurements and cannot be ranked interchangeably.
- Tencent, Sony, Microsoft and Nintendo are among the most commercially important gaming groups; NetEase, Take-Two, Valve and private investment funds are also influential. A 'top four' ranking depends on the period and the exact revenue definition.
- Acquisitions remain active: examples include Microsoft's Activision Blizzard purchase, Tencent's Ubisoft Vantage Studios minority stake and the 2026 buyout of Electronic Arts by PIF/Silver Lake/Affinity. These deals do **not** show that Sony, Microsoft, Nintendo and Tencent themselves are merging.

## 2. Who owns CVC and Haveli?

**CVC**: A publicly listed global private-markets manager, with multiple shareholders and fund structures. Its **listed parent-company shareholder register is distinct from the limited partners investing in CVC-managed funds**. It would be incorrect to say that a shareholder of CVC plc personally owns an equivalent share of Jagex. Rolly van Rappard, Rob Lucas and other executives have held senior roles; a previous conversation's precise investor percentages were not re-audited here, and should not be presented as current facts without reviewing current filings.

**Haveli**: Privately held, technology-focused investment manager founded by Brian Sheth. Its outside fund investors and underlying legal ownership interests are not equivalent to the founder's personal wealth or direct ownership of Jagex. [Official team](https://www.haveliinvestments.com/team/).

**Other CVC investments discussed:** Authentic Brands Group (CVC funds took a significant **minority stake**, with a **US$12.7bn enterprise valuation** at announcement in 2021), and Hargreaves Lansdown (acquired in 2025 by a consortium including CVC funds, Nordic Capital and an ADIA entity). These are **examples of substantial deals, not a verified ranking of CVC's three largest current holdings**. [CVC/Authentic Brands](https://www.cvc.com/media/news/2021/2021-11-22-cvc-fund-viii-and-hps-investment-partners-to-acquire-stakes-in-authentic-brands-group/) · [Hargreaves Lansdown ownership](https://www.hl.co.uk/investor-relations). Recordati was also mentioned in the conversation, but its current transaction state/value was not sufficiently audited for a 'top three' label.

**Scale trap:** CVC, Blackstone, Vista and Haveli may publish different categories of *assets under management* (fee-paying vs total AUM, commitments, invested capital). Those are neither the companies' own valuation nor gaming revenue. The earlier conversation's comparisons were indicative and should not be reproduced as a definitive apples-to-apples ranking.

## 3. Treasure Hunter: what players objected to and what changed

Treasure Hunter was **RuneScape 3**, not Old School RuneScape. Players used daily/earned or **purchased** keys for random rewards, including XP-related items. Longstanding criticism focused on:

1. Paid progression and potential erosion of the meaning of skill achievements.
2. Random-reward mechanics, gambling-like incentives and risks of overspending.
3. Time-limited promotional pressure and perceived unequal progression between players who spend and those who do not.
4. Effects on game balance, economy and trust.

**Verified timeline:**
- 12 November 2025: Jagex announced the outcome of the community vote (**124,985 votes recorded**), and its plan to remove Treasure Hunter.
- 12 January 2026: Treasure Hunter key sales ended.
- 19 January 2026: Treasure Hunter was removed; **225 direct-XP and skilling-related shop items** were removed from sale. This **did not imply all monetisation ceased**; RuneCoins remained for cosmetics.

Primary evidence: [Jagex/RuneScape vote and shutdown statement](https://secure.runescape.com/m%3Dnews/a-new-era-for-runescape-begins-january-19-2026) · [Jagex RS25 update](https://www.jagex.com/news/an-ambitious-new-era-dawns-for-runescape-as-it-marks-its-25th-anniversary).

**Interpretation, not disclosed management motive:** Giving up a monetisation mechanic can support long-term player trust and retention, but a direct causal link to any particular investor's decision-making is **not proven**. Jagex's January 2026 announcement described record franchise investment, integrity work, support improvements and RuneScape: Dragonwilds expansion. [RS25](https://www.jagex.com/news/jagex-marks-runescape%E2%80%99s-25th-year-with-rs25-delivering-record-investment-a-dedicated-game-integrity-roadmap-new-game-modes-player-first-design-and-franchise-expansion).

## 6. Who owns Tencent?

Tencent is a listed Hong Kong technology and entertainment company founded in 1998 and led by co-founder **Ma Huateng ('Pony Ma')**, its chairman and CEO. It also operates WeChat and other major digital services.

A shareholder data snapshot **as of 30 June 2026** reported:
- **Prosus NV: 22.60%** (largest reported holder).
- **Ma Huateng: 8.85%**.
- **Others: 68.55%**.

[HK stock information with dates](https://www.etnet.com.hk/www/eng/stocks/realtime/quote_ci_brief.php?code=00700). Prosus is a Dutch-listed investor with a major historical connection to South Africa's Naspers; a **22.6% equity holding is not a 100% ownership claim or automatically a majority voting interest**.

Tencent is especially powerful in mobile and online/live-service gaming, and via Riot, Supercell and minority positions such as Epic. This helps explain why critic-score 'all-time classics' lists under-represent its market reach.

## 10. Implications for the JoshRS project

1. **Rights remain separate from corporate analysis.** Buying shares or buying a studio does not grant the public or JoshRS the right to distribute proprietary RuneScape IP. JoshRS must continue to separate historically referential design notes, licensed/open-source code, original content and rights-restricted game assets.
2. **Keep the game's independent implementation boundaries.** Do not construe this paper as a request to modify the JParris Desktop active world, accounts, runtime or player saves.
3. **Commercial context can inform design choices**, especially the long-term value of player trust, legitimate progression and avoiding pay-to-win/random-reward incentives, but private meeting motives cannot be asserted.
4. **Watch announcements, not speculation:** for corporate changes, prefer Jagex press releases, investor filings and official acquisition-completion notices; label reported valuations and hypothetical IP estimates distinctly.
5. **For future comparisons, use a data dictionary** defining revenue, enterprise value, equity value, AUM, IP-only appraisal, shareholding and original-vs-current ownership before drawing charts.
